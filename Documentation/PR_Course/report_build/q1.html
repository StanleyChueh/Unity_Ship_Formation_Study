1. Topic

Learning a camera tracker from data: maximum-likelihood noise estimation and Bayesian decision rules for target tracking under fast vertical image motion, on a drone and an unmanned surface vessel (USV).

Background. Our group tracks one target with a camera on two platforms, using the same pipeline: YOLOv8 detection → Lucas-Kanade optical flow → Kalman filter. My teammate tracks a flying drone. I track the leader boat of a simulated USV fleet that keeps formation using only a camera. Both fail when the target's box moves up and down quickly in the image. The drone climbs and drops; the boat camera pitches in waves. On my USV logs, YOLO's missed detections rise from 1.8% to 14.7% as the box's vertical speed rises from 0–2 to 20–40 px per step.

Problem. The tracker's key parameters are hand-tuned for one platform: the process noise Q, the measurement noise R = 60·(0.5/confidence)², and a heuristic gate that decides which box is the target. Hand-tuned values do not transfer. On the boat, the drone tracker is worse than raw YOLO: 73% vs 85% of frames tracked with IoU ≥ 0.5 in clear water.

Approach (course methods).

  1. Maximum-likelihood estimation (Ch. 3-1). R is the Gaussian MLE of the detection error, Σ̂ = (1/n)Σ(ek − μ̂)(ek − μ̂)t. Q is the value that maximises the likelihood of the filter's innovations:
    Q̂ = arg maxQ Σk ln N(νk; 0, Sk(Q)),   Sk = HPk|k−1Ht + R
    When ground truth exists, Q also has the textbook closed form Q̂ = (1/n)Σ wkwkt, with wk = xk+1 − Fxk.
  2. Minimum-risk Bayes rule (Ch. 2) to accept or reject each detected box. Features: confidence, Mahalanobis distance, size change. Accept if
    p(x|ω1)/p(x|ω2) > [(λ12 − λ22)/(λ21 − λ11)] · P(ω2)/P(ω1)
    where accepting a false box costs more than rejecting a true one.
  3. PCA, Fisher LDA and the Hughes effect (Ch. 3-2, Ch. 1) to choose and visualise the features, and a measured curve of accuracy against dimension and training-set size.
Preliminary result (offline replay against ground truth; Q learned on training data only). Q chosen by maximum likelihood is 10× the hand-tuned value on the boat. That matches the frame rates: 10 Hz against 30 fps means about 3× the motion per step, so about 9× the variance. With it, the tracker is better than the hand-tuned one on 64/64 USV sequences (median +13.7 points, Wilcoxon p = 4e-12). In clear water it reaches 88%, against 85% for YOLO alone. On the drone videos with a slow detector it gains +25 and +25 points (95% CIs exclude zero), and +24 points on a held-out video of a different drone. In pre-registered extreme waves (sea 4–5, heavy rain, dark storm) it still beats the hand-tuned tracker on 28/30 new sequences (p = 3.5e-06). Against raw YOLO it ties or comes close in clear and rainy huge waves, but trails by about 10 points in the dark storm: that limit is what the final project will address.

4. Final product

  1. A Python module shared by both platforms that replaces the hand-tuned constants: MLE noise model (R, Q), Bayes gate and feature analysis.
  2. Labelled evaluation data: USV camera sequences with exact simulator ground truth, and drone videos with checked pseudo ground truth.
  3. A reproducible offline replay that scores every tracker version against ground truth, with statistical tests.
  4. Side-by-side detection videos (YOLO only | hand-tuned | learned) for both platforms.
